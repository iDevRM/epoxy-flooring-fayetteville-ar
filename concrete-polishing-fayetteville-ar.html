Polished Concrete Floors in Fayetteville, AR

Mechanically ground and chemically densified — a hard, light-reflecting floor with no coating layer that can peel, blister, or need recoating.

Concrete polishing in Fayetteville turns the slab you already own into the finished floor. There is no coating involved — diamond tooling grinds the surface through progressively finer grits while a chemical densifier hardens the concrete itself. The result reflects light, sweeps clean, and cannot delaminate, because there is nothing bonded on top of it to let go. It is why so many Northwest Arkansas warehouses, retail buildouts, breweries, and shop floors end up polished instead of coated.

A densified, multi-pass polish — the slab is the floor.

What Does Concrete Polishing Involve?

We start by taking the floor down with coarse metal-bond diamonds, usually somewhere between 30 and 80 grit depending on how much aggregate you want showing and how flat the slab is. That first pass decides the look. Stop early and you get a cream finish where the surface paste stays intact. Go deeper and you expose sand, then stone.

Once the cut is set, we fill cracks and pinholes, then apply a lithium or sodium silicate densifier. That soaks in and reacts with free lime in the concrete to create a harder, denser surface. From there it is resin-bond passes through the grits — 100, 200, 400, and up to 800 or 1500 if you want real gloss — with a guard applied near the end to resist staining.

Big open slabs move fast. A 5,000 square foot warehouse floor is usually a few days. Small rooms with lots of edge work take proportionally longer, because every wall line gets hand-ground with an edger.

When Is Polished Concrete the Right Call?

Warehouse and distribution space is the obvious one, and Northwest Arkansas has no shortage of it between Springdale, Lowell, and Bentonville. Forklift traffic destroys thin coatings over time. It does not destroy the slab.

Retail and restaurant buildouts are the second. A polished floor reads as intentional, handles carts and heels, and costs less per square foot than most flooring you would install over the top.

Third is anyone who has already replaced a peeling coating once and does not want a third round. If your slab is sound and you do not need chemical resistance or a specific color, polishing removes the failure mode entirely.

Skip it if the slab is badly cracked or has been patched in a dozen colors. Grinding will make those repairs more visible, not less.

Why Do Fayetteville Slabs Polish Unevenly?

The usual cause is an over-troweled surface. When a finishing crew works a slab too hard or too long, the top layer ends up dense and glassy but weak underneath, and the diamonds cut through it in patches. You end up with shiny areas next to dull ones and no amount of extra passes fixes it.

The second cause is slab flatness. Grinders ride the high spots. On a floor with real dips, the high areas expose aggregate while the low areas stay cream, and the aggregate pattern maps out every imperfection in the original pour. This shows up often on older Fayetteville commercial slabs poured before laser screeds were standard.

Third is moisture, which matters less here than with coatings but still matters. A slab with heavy vapor drive can push efflorescence up through a polished surface and leave a white haze. We test before quoting, same as we do on a coating job.

What Affects the Cost of Polished Concrete in Fayetteville?

Plan on $4 to $9 per square foot for most Fayetteville jobs. Nationally, a basic cream-finish polish runs $3 to $8 per square foot, a salt-and-pepper polish $4 to $10, and a high-gloss multi-pass finish $6 to $15.

  • Gloss level: a soft matte finish is the cheapest option; every additional grit pass adds labor
  • Aggregate exposure: a deep cut to expose stone means more time on coarse metals and more diamond wear
  • Slab repair: crack chasing and patching typically adds $100 to $800 on a mid-size floor; slab leveling is a separate job at $3–$12 per square foot
  • Dye or stain: dye adds $1–$3 per square foot, single-color stain $3–$5
  • Layout: columns, alcoves, and heavy edge work add 10–30% in labor because it is all hand-grinding
  • Coating removal: if there is glue, paint, or old epoxy on the slab, budget $1.50–$3.50 per square foot to get it off first

Larger floors price better per square foot. A 2,000 square foot space typically runs $12,000 to $18,000 nationally; the same job in Northwest Arkansas usually comes in under that.

Polished Concrete vs. Epoxy: Which Do You Need?

Buy polished concrete when the slab is sound, the space is large, and you want a floor that never needs recoating. It handles forklifts and foot traffic, reflects light so you may need fewer fixtures, and has no bond line to fail under vapor pressure.

Buy epoxy when you need chemical resistance, a specific color, a seamless surface with coved base for wash-down, or when the slab is too rough to polish cleanly. A commercial kitchen or a mechanic bay wants a coating. A retail floor usually does not.

One thing contractors gloss over: polished concrete is not maintenance-free. It wants a neutral-pH auto-scrubber routine and a guard reapplied every few years in high-traffic lanes. It is less maintenance than a coating, not none.

Frequently Asked Questions — Polished Concrete in Fayetteville, AR

How much does polished concrete cost in Fayetteville, AR?

Polished concrete in Fayetteville runs $4 to $9 per square foot for most jobs. A basic cream-finish polish sits at the low end, a salt-and-pepper grind in the middle, and a high-gloss multi-pass polish at the top. Slab repair before polishing is quoted separately.

Is polished concrete better than epoxy for a warehouse?

For a large warehouse slab in good condition, usually yes. Polished concrete has no coating layer, so there is nothing to delaminate under forklift traffic or moisture pressure, and it never needs recoating. Epoxy wins where you need chemical resistance, a specific color, or a seamless surface for wash-down.

Does polished concrete need to be sealed?

True mechanically polished concrete does not need a topical sealer. The densifier chemically hardens the slab surface and the progressive grit passes close the pores. A grind-and-seal floor is a different product and does need its sealer refreshed every few years.

Can any concrete slab in Fayetteville be polished?

Most can, but not all should. A slab with heavy spalling, wide moving cracks, or a soft over-troweled surface will not take a consistent polish, and grinding exposes aggregate unevenly. We check hardness and flatness first and tell you honestly if a coating is the better buy.
