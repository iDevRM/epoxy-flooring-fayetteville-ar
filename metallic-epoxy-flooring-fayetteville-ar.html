Metallic Epoxy Flooring in Fayetteville, AR

A hand-poured floor, made once, in a room. Priced like the craft labor it is — and worth understanding properly before you buy one.

Metallic epoxy flooring in Fayetteville is the only product we sell where the person doing the work matters more than the product in the bucket. Reflective pigment gets suspended in clear resin, poured, and then moved with squeegees and air while it is still liquid. Whatever happens in that window is permanent. The same materials in two different pairs of hands produce two different floors, which is why quotes vary so widely and why this page spends more time on choosing an installer than on chemistry.

Poured once, worked wet, permanent when it sets.

Why Two Quotes on the Same Room Differ by Thousands

Once the resin is mixed, a clock starts. You have somewhere around fifteen to thirty minutes of working time before it thickens past the point where pigment will still move, and that number shrinks on a warm day. Everything artistic about the floor has to happen inside that window.

The consequences are all economic. A larger room needs more hands on the floor at once, not more days. A crew of one will either rush the room or lose the pattern halfway across it. Installers who have poured a hundred of these charge more per hour and finish with fewer dead spots, because they know how far a batch will spread before it stops taking direction.

So when one bid comes in at $3,000 and another at $5,200 for the same garage, the gap is rarely material cost. It is crew size, experience, and how much of the slab prep is included. Ask each bidder how many people will be on the floor during the pour. The answers will explain the numbers.

Choosing a Color You Will Still Like in Five Years

The base coat underneath does at least half the work. Identical pigment over charcoal reads as storm and shadow; over pearl it reads as marble; over a warm grey it goes bronze. Customers routinely pick a pigment name and are surprised by the result because nobody showed them the base first.

Lighting decides the rest. A metallic floor is a reflective surface, so it takes on the character of what is above it. Under warm residential bulbs in a finished basement off Wedington, a copper blend looks rich. The same floor under 5000K shop lights looks harsh and green. We ask what fixtures are going in before finalizing anything.

Two practical rules. Look at physical sample boards in the actual room, at the time of day you will use it most, never on a phone screen. And be careful with high-contrast blends — dramatic photographs beautifully and gets tiring to live with. The floors people are still happy with years later tend to be the quieter ones.

Where It Belongs in a Northwest Arkansas Home or Business

Finished basements are the strongest fit locally. Plenty of Fayetteville homes are built into a hillside with a walkout lower level that gets used as a family room or gym, and a metallic floor turns that slab into the nicest surface in the house for less than tile would cost.

Then showrooms, salons, tasting rooms, boutiques — anywhere a customer stands still and looks around. In a retail space the floor does work that signage cannot.

Garages are a maybe. A garage that functions as a hangout, gym, or display space is a fine candidate. A garage where you actually turn wrenches is not, and we will say so before quoting.

One underrated use: a slab that has been patched so many times it will never look uniform again. Solid color paint frames every repair. A mottled metallic pour hides all of them, which sometimes makes it the cheapest honest option rather than the most expensive one.

The Maintenance Reality Nobody Mentions at the Sale

It is a durable floor, not an indestructible one, and it is the least repairable product we install.

Scratches show more than they would on flake, because the surface is smooth and reflective. Grit dragged in on shoes is the usual culprit, so a mat at the door does more for the floor's appearance than any cleaning product.

There is no invisible spot repair. The pattern happened in one pour, and a patched area will always announce itself. Serious damage means recoating the section or the room.

It is slick when wet. We blend a fine polymer grit into the clear on anything that sees water — a basement with a laundry, a garage where cars come in during a spring storm. It barely changes the shine and it prevents the one complaint we take seriously.

Cleaning is a dust mop and a pH-neutral cleaner. No vinegar, no citrus degreaser, nothing abrasive.

What It Costs and What Moves the Number

Expect $7 to $12 per square foot installed in the Fayetteville market. A two-car garage typically lands between $3,200 and $5,400. National pricing for the same system runs $8 to $15 per square foot, and we sit under it because overhead here is lower, not because steps get skipped.

Getting the slab clean. Old adhesive, spalling, or a previous coating that has to be ground off adds $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot. Preparation is 25 to 40 percent of the labor on a metallic job.

Moisture control. Hillside lots and Ozark limestone move water sideways, and older slabs in Washington County often have a compromised vapor barrier. If the test comes back high, a barrier primer runs $1.00 to $1.75 per square foot. Skipping it is how a beautiful floor blisters in its second spring.

Number of colors. One pigment over a base is the low end. Three-color blends with pulled veining take longer and demand more skill.

Room geometry. Long open runs pour efficiently. Small rooms, corners and columns cost more per square foot, because the working time does not get longer just because the room got smaller.

Every estimate is written and itemized with the moisture reading printed on it. If the slab tests dry, the mitigation line disappears.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are metallic floor quotes so far apart?

Because you are buying labor hours from a person, not a product from a shelf. The pigment has to be worked before the resin thickens, roughly a fifteen to thirty minute window per section, so the crew size and the installer's experience set the price more than the room size does.

Can I pick my metallic floor color from a photo online?

Pick a direction from a photo, then decide from a physical sample under your own lights. Metallic pigment changes character completely depending on whether it sits over a black, grey or pearl base, and screens misrepresent both the depth and the tone. We bring boards to the estimate for this reason.

What happens if I gouge a metallic floor?

There is no invisible patch. The pattern was created in one continuous pour, so a repair reads as a repair. Deep damage is handled by recoating the whole area rather than spotting it, which is the main practical reason to keep metallic out of working shops.

How much does metallic epoxy cost in Fayetteville, AR?

Between $7 and $12 per square foot installed, so a two-car garage generally falls between $3,200 and $5,400. Getting an old coating off the slab first, or adding a moisture barrier if the concrete tests wet, are separate line items on top of that.
